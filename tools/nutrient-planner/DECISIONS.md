# Nutrient planner: design decisions

Tool-specific intent and decisions, newest first. Site-wide principles are in
[`docs/PRINCIPLES.md`](../../docs/PRINCIPLES.md).

## Intent

Help someone find a small number of cheap, everyday foods that together meet a day's nutrient recommendations, or
check a day they've planned themselves, including vegetarians and vegans who want to be sure nothing is missing,
and shoppers at ordinary UK supermarkets. It is for thinking about food, not a medical tool.

## Decisions

|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-18 | Thiamin, vitamin B6 and pantothenic acid from USDA and CoFID combined into one definition each, with an Assumption caveat shown with every such value: CoFID states the salt form (hydrochloride, calcium pantothenate), USDA doesn't; across the matched foods CoFID/USDA medians are 1.08, 0.96 and 1.00, with no sign of the 1.27, 1.22 and 1.09 factors a different basis would give | Owner's decision: combine on the data check, with the reason recorded and a disclaimer shown |
| 2026-11-18 | Blended values are stored per **definition**, not per nutrient name (e.g. energy with US Atwater factors and energy with EU factors are different components), and only combined within one definition; each table's definitions are recorded with the section of its documentation that states them (`pipeline/blend/components.csv`). Kept apart for USDA–CoFID: energy, carbohydrate, sugars, vitamin E, vitamin D for meat, and (for now) thiamin, vitamin B6 and pantothenic acid. Vitamin A converted both ways (RE and RAE) from published retinol and carotenoids | INFOODS/EuroFIR: a component determined differently is a different component. Thiamin, B6 and pantothenic acid: CoFID states the salt form they are expressed as, USDA doesn't, so the documents don't show they match (owner's rule: when in doubt, keep separate) |
| 2026-11-18 | Blend builder (beta, not used by the planner yet): median and range per definition for the 142 USDA–CoFID same/equivalent foods; missing, trace and borrowed values left out with the reason shown; a CoFID record whose main data reference cites USDA counts once; two-fold differences flagged, not dropped; salt and EPA+DHA derived from blended parts; full audit trail per value | Owner's design: traceable to the exact source, file and rule, with a plain-English explanation per food |
| 2026-11-18 | All 2,244 planner foods matched to CoFID. The 2,128 not reviewed one by one are decided by rule v2, which applies the confirmed review rule mechanically (same name after synonyms, no differing facet, one-sided details only where the composition check would catch an error, composition agrees on at least 3 nutrients, not a mixture, exactly one record fits); labelled "Automated rule v2, without manual human verification (beta)". Result: 139 same, 3 equivalent, 1,424 similar, 678 none | Owner: proceed to all planner foods; automated reviews may be used with a beta label. A deterministic rule is checkable and re-runs when the evidence changes |
| 2026-11-18 | The review rule for matching is confirmed (same only if facets agree and composition agrees, or a raw single food within natural variation; any possible product difference → kept separate). Matches reviewed automatically may be used, labelled "automated review, without manual human verification (beta)", and the blended list is marked beta wherever it is shown | Owner's decision |
| 2026-11-18 | Blended list step 3 begun on USDA and CoFID: concepts = the 116 common foods; each matched to a CoFID record with grade same / equivalent / similar / none, a reason, the reviewer and the fingerprint of the evidence reviewed. Review rule: same only if facets agree and composition agrees (or a raw single food within natural variation); any possible product difference → similar (kept separate). Result: 63 same, 3 equivalent, 35 similar, 15 none (automated review, without manual human verification; beta). Matching sees every CoFID record (2,887) | Owner: blend only the same food, keep doubtful ones separate; decisions must stay traceable and be re-reviewed if their evidence changes |
| 2026-11-18 | Blended food list (paused feature) planned for every food in every table, built in small steps: (1) describe foods by facets, (2) the other tables, (3) matching with review, (4) component definitions, (5) blending, (6–8) data delivery, solver and display. Point value = median of the tables that have the same food; the range is always shown; when in doubt foods stay separate, named with a source tag. Hand decisions in CSV (one file per table), generated output in JSON | Owner's aim: take the best of all tables, traceable to the exact source, file and rule. FAO/INFOODS food-matching guidelines; INFOODS/EuroFIR component definitions (carbohydrate, fibre, fat, energy, folate are not comparable across methods); CSV is diffable and opens in a spreadsheet |
| 2026-11-18 | Step 1 done for the planner's foods: `pipeline/blend/` describes all 2,244 USDA foods and CoFID's 1,072 by facets (state, cooking, preservation, salt, sugar, packing medium, fortification, fat, part, form, bone, cut, variety, origin...), from a reviewed vocabulary; unrecognised phrases are kept verbatim, never guessed. Permanent food ids `<table>:<code>` without the version | Matching needs a common description first; ids must survive new releases |
| 2026-11-18 | One food file per national table (`pipeline/tables/`), each using its own table only; first: UK CoFID 2021 (1,072 foods, 84 of the 116 common foods). CoFID codes "Tr" and "N" stay missing and are labelled; carbohydrate and sugars are kept as CoFID publishes them (monosaccharide equivalents) with the definition recorded, not converted; vitamin A (retinol equivalents) and folate (total folate) keep CoFID's definitions. Not yet offered on the page | Owner chose a per-table choice first (blending later). Converting carbohydrate needs a judgement CoFID leaves to the user (mainly starch or not), so the published value is kept and labelled instead |
| 2026-11-18 | Owner decisions: published estimates and derived values (Japan's bracketed values, BLS "borrowing", recipe calculations, etc.) may be used, each labelled as its source describes it; Japanese food names shown with our English translation (marked as translated) and MEXT's English source name; Korea KFCDb 9.3 (English) used; BLS 4.0 the default table for EU targets | Owner's instructions, 17-18 Nov |
| 2026-11-18 | Methods page reorganised: a short "In brief" list, each topic in a collapsible section with sub-sections, limitations always visible | Owner asked for easier navigation; limitations stay in view because principle 2 asks for them to be stated plainly |
| 2026-11-17 | Up to 50 options (was 5), with a Stop button that keeps the options found so far | The search runs in the visitor's browser, so the limit only cost them choice; owner asked not to restrict it artificially. 50 stops a damaged or hostile link from starting an endless search |
| 2026-11-17 | Options are made varied: each shares fewer than half of its foods with any earlier option and no food is used in more than half of the options (at least two); the rules are loosened step by step only if an option would otherwise meet the targets worse than the first by more than 2% of one target, and the option then says so | Owner noticed red lentils and sweet potatoes in nearly every option. The old rule (change a third of the foods) let 4 of 6 foods repeat and a base of the same foods run through all options. With the common foods, every option still met every target in testing |
| 2026-11-17 | Other food tables are linked to planner foods through a reviewed crosswalk (`pipeline/crosswalk/`): a script proposes candidates by name, cooking state and composition (water, protein, fat, sodium, calcium); a reviewer records accept / check / none with a note; only "accept" links may be merged, only for nutrients USDA lacks, each value keeping its source. First pass: CoFID 2021 for the 116 common foods (66 accept, 22 check, 28 none). Not merged yet | Automatic name matching gave plausible but wrong pairs (Fontina to Colby, salted to unsalted), which would borrow another food's value (principle 2). Matching the planner's own food concepts, as FAO/INFOODS recommends, links far more foods correctly |
| 2026-11-17 | Wider selection reviewed by the site owner: the odd US blend "Oil, corn, peanut, and olive" removed (plain olive oil is already a common food); cooked bacon and smoked haddock added by name (new `also` list in `wider_selection.json`) | The owner asked for olive oil and for British staples; sausages, black pudding, crumpets, jam, lard and similar are not in the USDA SR Legacy data used, so can't be added without a different source |
| 2026-11-17 | Missing food data is shown as NA (no amount, no chart axis) or as an "at least" lower bound, never as zero; results the missing data could change are "unclear"; suggestions use known values only; EPA + DHA and niacin equivalents in `foods.json` are left missing unless every part is known; the "no data" chip is now "not checked" | Owner's requirement: no invented or zero-filled values (principle 2); zero would flatter limits and could hide a shortfall |
| 2026-11-17 | Reference tables split into one file per standard (`pipeline/targets/`), each row length checked when the script runs. Generated targets unchanged (byte-identical) | One file can be checked against one document; a row with a missing value now stops the build instead of being cut short silently |
| 2026-11-17 | Code split into small modules: calculations in plain modules with tests, page code in `web/ui/` with one file per section (map in the README). No change in behaviour | Easier for people and AI to review; a rule can be found and checked in one place |
| 2026-11-17 | "Wider selection" preset: about 400 ordinary foods on top of the common list (`pipeline/wider_selection.json`; list in `data/foods/wider_selection.md`) | Variety without the odd picks of "Everything". The list is the maintainers' judgement, for the owner to review |
| 2026-11-17 | Mild preference for familiar foods in suggestions, capped at 1% of one target per plan; stated on the methods page | "Everything" picked dried lychees and bread sticks because the optimiser only sees nutrients |
| 2026-11-17 | When a long food list times out, also search the common foods and keep the better plan | A time-limited search over 400+ foods gave a worse plan than the common foods alone |
| 2026-11-17 | Vitamin D is reported on its own line and left out of the "targets met" count unless the visitor asks to plan for it | Otherwise almost every result read "short on vitamin D", burying shortfalls the plan was actually trying to avoid. Its amount, status and the supplement advice are still shown |
| 2026-11-17 | US-fortified products are flagged and left out of the "Everything" preset (still selectable one by one) | A UK shopper's version may not have the added nutrients, so a plan built on them could mislead |
| 2026-11-17 | Children: a warning (stronger under 5) and portion limits scaled by calories relative to a 2,000 kcal day. No minimum age | Adult portion caps gave toddler plans with 130 g of kale; the scaling is a rule of thumb and is labelled as one |
| 2026-11-17 | Suggestions leave out foods the NHS says a group should avoid (liver in pregnancy; shark, swordfish, marlin in pregnancy and under 16); the builder warns | The vitamin A limit alone only capped liver at about 30 g |
| 2026-11-17 | Suggestions don't chase vitamin D unless the visitor ticks a box (off by default); the day's vitamin D and the supplement notice are still shown | It forced 200–300 g of salmon or 200 g of UV mushrooms into most plans, against the 8 Nov decision below |
| 2026-11-17 | Oily fish (NHS list) share one cap of 140 g a day in suggestions | One plan had 430 g in a day beside a note giving the NHS weekly limits |
| 2026-11-17 | US saturated fat limit from the Dietary Guidelines (under 10% of calories, age 2+); no saturated fat limit under EU | The US tables have none; EFSA says "as low as possible" with no number, and borrowing a number would overstate what EFSA says |
| 2026-11-17 | Targets set as a share of energy are judged against each plan's own calories | A fixed gram amount lets a low-calorie plan exceed the percentage unnoticed |
| 2026-11-17 | Carbohydrate is USDA's "by difference" value minus fibre | Targets and food labels count carbohydrate without fibre |
| 2026-11-10 | Amino acid targets use the WHO safe level (Table 23 average + 24%, §8.4) | Matches the basis of every other target (covers nearly everyone) |
| 2026-11-10 | US calories use exact Dietary Guidelines Table A2-2 values for age and sex, band ±10% | The earlier secondary-source bands were inaccurate |
| 2026-11-09 | EU (EFSA) standard added alongside UK and US; other standards shown as thin outlines on charts, on by default, toggleable | Compare major standards without clutter |
| 2026-11-09 | Safety upper limits borrowed from the US tables for every standard, and said so | The UK and EU tables used give no complete set |
| 2026-11-09 | Diet defaults to "No restriction"; vegetarian and vegan are options | Most visitors aren't restricted |
| 2026-11-09 | Radar charts: food-label categories on top ("At a glance"), detail charts for vitamins, minerals, amino acids and fats in collapsibles, computed only when opened | Minimal by default; recognised categories |
| 2026-11-09 | Radar scale linear from 0, extending past 150% to fit; "Zoom to target" marks values beyond the edge | No misleading clipping |
| 2026-11-09 | Two modes: "Suggest foods for me" (optimiser) and "Build my own" (live update) | Both planning and checking |
| 2026-11-09 | Inputs reduced to sex, age and weight; standard, life stage, calories and target tweaks under "Fine-tune targets" | Simple by default |
| 2026-11-09 | ~116 curated common foods on by default; ~2,100 more from USDA, off by default, in a filterable hierarchy | Everyday foods first, more on request |
| 2026-11-08 | Vitamin D, B12, EPA/DHA and iodine shown as notices (sun/supplements, fortified foods, data gaps), not forced through food | Food alone often can't meet them sensibly; advice comes from the NHS |
| 2026-11-08 | Visitors can override any target; overridden values are marked as differing from the recommendation | Freedom without hiding the official value |
| 2026-11-08 | Food prices ignored for now | Price data isn't available reliably; possible later |
| 2026-11-08 | Optimiser: mixed-integer programme (HiGHS, in the browser) minimising total relative shortfall, never exceeding upper limits or the calorie band; practical amounts (10 g, half an egg, a slice) | Fewest foods, practical portions, transparent method |
| 2026-11-08 | Food data: local snapshot of USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (public domain), refreshed quarterly | No UK dataset with comparable coverage and licence; resilience |

## Known gaps and ideas (not promises)

- How well nutrients are absorbed isn't modelled yet, for example iron from plants, phytates reducing zinc, or ALA
  converting to EPA and DHA.
- When "Everything" foods are on, the planner can pick unusual foods; preferring everyday foods could help.
- US fortification differs from the UK (for example milk with vitamin D), so fortified foods should be checked against
  their labels.
- Foundation Foods data could be added once USDA's download includes `food.csv` again.
